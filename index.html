1. Agile Team Charter

Evidence

Reflection — STAR Method

Situation

During the Velour Essence project, our team needed to establish a clear purpose, vision and structure to guide the development of our custom perfume business. The Agile Team Charter helped us understand what we wanted to achieve and how each team member would contribute towards the project.

Task

My role in the team was Research and Marketing. My responsibility was to research competitors, gather customer feedback and help determine how we would present and promote Velour Essence to our target audience.

Action

I contributed to the team by understanding my responsibilities and how they connected to the overall project objectives. I learned that my role was not only about marketing the product but also about understanding the market, identifying customer needs and ensuring that the business remained relevant to its target audience. I also understood how the different roles worked together, from the Product Owner establishing the vision to the Scrum Master organising the process, the Designer creating the user experience and the Developer building the technical solution.

Result

The Agile Team Charter gave me a better understanding of how an Agile team is structured and why clearly defined responsibilities are important. I learned that successful project development requires collaboration, communication and accountability, as every team member contributes towards a shared goal. This helped me understand the importance of my Research and Marketing role in connecting the business with its customers and supporting the overall success of Velour Essence.

2. User Stories and Tasks

Evidence

Reflection — STAR Method

Situation

During the Velour Essence project, our team needed to understand what customers would expect from our custom perfume website and identify the features that would make their experience easier. We organised these needs into six epics: Custom Scent Builder, Label and Bottle Personalization, Checkout and Payments, Account and Order Tracking, Admin and Operations, and Marketing and Engagement.

Task

My responsibility as part of Research and Marketing was to consider the needs of our target audience and understand how customer requirements could be translated into user stories. I also had to make sure the marketing needs of the brand, such as promotion and customer engagement, were included in the stories.

Action

I learned that user stories describe a feature from the customer's point of view using the format "As a customer, I want… so that…". The "so that" part is important because it explains the value the feature gives to the customer. From my research and marketing perspective, I looked at how the stories matched real customer needs. For example, the stories about a live preview of the custom blend and suggested complementary notes help customers who are not perfume experts to feel confident about what they are ordering. The stories about custom labels and small, medium and large bottle sizes show that customers want a personal, giftable product that fits their budget. The checkout stories, such as seeing the price update while customising and having card and EFT payment options, show that customers want no surprises and the freedom to pay the way they prefer.

I also focused on the stories that supported the business and its marketing. The admin story about viewing and exporting customer survey results shows how customer feedback can be used to adjust pricing and scent offerings. In Epic 6, the story where customers share their custom bottle design on social media helps the brand grow through the customers themselves, and the story where the marketing team schedules and publishes launch content helps our campaign run on time across different channels. I also learned that breaking each user story into smaller tasks makes the work easier to understand, organise and complete.

Result

This helped me understand how customer needs influence the development of a system. I learned that user stories give the team a clear direction because they explain what needs to be developed and what value it should provide. I also learned that my Research and Marketing role is important in the user stories because research shows what customers actually want, and marketing makes sure the product can be promoted and shared. This showed me that a good product is built around the customer from the start and not only around the technical features.
